# IPIP interface direction

User-approved direction: calm editorial style, ink-blue text, bright blue accent, generous spacing, large readable questions.

Reading: a questionnaire for personal reflection. ENERGY 1 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 1.

- Ink-blue text keeps long-form questions comfortable to read; blue identifies chosen answers and primary actions.
- Libre Caslon Display gives question statements the pace of a printed inventory; DM Sans keeps labels and controls readable.
- A narrow progress rail separates navigation from the current question without exposing trait assignments that could influence responses.
- The numbered trait index repeats in results to connect the five measured dimensions with the resulting profile.
- White panels group the version choice and answer options; borders define hit areas without stacking shadows.
- Rounded radio indicators communicate choice; modest rectangular button and panel corners preserve hierarchy.
- Spacing separates the statement, response scale, and continuation action, reducing accidental selections.
- No decorative imagery is necessary for the questionnaire. The favicon is a typographic initial for identifying its browser tab; moon/sun icons represent the theme setting.
- Arrows appear only on actions that advance the questionnaire or open the original source.
- Motion is limited to hover and selection colors, respecting reduced-motion preferences.
- A light/dark switch accommodates different reading environments using corresponding high-contrast tokens.

Scoring is transparent: actual public-domain item keys, reversed negative items, facet and domain sums. The 0–100 display is the possible score-range position, never a population percentile. Question order is interleaved by facet and domain, not presented as an exact recreation of the original CGI administration order.

The reading-help panel separates the original statement from its editorial explanation. Supporting text under each response translates the scale labels into everyday language. Results pair two end-of-scale examples at desktop sizes and stack them on phones. A lightly tinted reflection prompt gives each scale a practical reading pause. Expanded facet rows carry their own meaning and examples because the user requested help with every result point.

Results now use summary-first reading order: a compact title, five comparable score rows, optional facet statistics, then explanations. Consistent bar scales support comparison without implying population percentiles or good/bad categories. Facet disclosure avoids forcing readers through 30 rows before comparing the five main scores, with one control to reveal everything. Explanation shortcuts scroll and transfer focus without changing the URL fragment, preserving shared links. Mobile rows prioritize names and exact values and omit decorative bars when space is limited.
